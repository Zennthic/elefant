What is Elefant?

Elefant is a knowledge base and AI memory platform that gives your organisation a private, structured place to store what it knows — written documentation in the Wiki, and structured facts in the Memory layer that your AI assistants can search, read, and add to in real time.

Unlike cloud AI tools that store your context on third-party servers, Elefant keeps your data under your control. Every document, every memory, and every conversation context lives on infrastructure you own. Elefant connects to AI assistants (Claude, Cursor, and others) over the standard Model Context Protocol (MCP) — an open spec, not a proprietary lock-in.

📖 Wiki Markdown documents organised in folders. Your team's SOPs, runbooks, decisions, reference material. Readable and searchable by your AI assistants.
🧠 Memories Structured facts, decisions, and preferences. Your AI assistants can search and add memories in real time — building persistent context across sessions.
🔒 Data Ownership Your content never leaves your infrastructure. No vendor has access to your wiki or memories. You can export and move everything at any time.
🔌 Platform-Agnostic Connects via MCP — the open standard supported by Claude, Cursor, Windsurf, and others. Change your AI provider without losing your data.

Your Organisation at a Glance

Your Elefant workspace is structured as an Organisation (Org). As an Org Admin, you have full control over users, teams, groups, and the settings for your Org. You do not have access to other organisations on the platform.

Role Hierarchy

RoleWhat they can do
Platform Admin Manages the platform itself — creates orgs, handles billing, cannot see your content.
Org Admin That's you. Manages users, teams, groups, API tokens, and wiki spaces within your org. Full access to your org's content.
User Can read and write wiki content and memories within their allowed spaces. Cannot manage other users or settings.

Admin Console

Your admin console is at https://your-domain.com/editor/admin.html. You'll find all management tools in the tabs along the top: Users, Teams, Groups, Invites, API Tokens, Wiki Spaces, and Settings.

Tip
The regular Wiki & Memories view at https://your-domain.com/ is where your users spend most of their time. The Admin Console is a separate page for management tasks only.

User Management & Onboarding

All users join Elefant via a one-time invite link that you generate from the Admin Console. There is no self-registration; every user must be explicitly invited.

Inviting a New User

  1. 1
    Open the Admin Console → Invites tab.
  2. 2
    Click Create Invite. Enter the new user's email address and select an initial Team and/or Group (optional — you can adjust later).
  3. 3
    Copy the invite URL that appears. Send it to the new user via email, Slack, or any other channel.
  4. 4
    The user opens the link, sets their password, and is taken through MFA enrolment on first login.
  5. 5
    Once they complete onboarding, they appear in the Users tab. You can assign them to teams and groups from there.
Invite link expiry
Invite links are one-time use and expire after 7 days. If a user hasn't accepted within that window, generate a new invite. Old invite links are invalidated automatically.

Managing Existing Users

In the Users tab you can:

  • View all users and their current role, team, and group memberships
  • Promote a user to Org Admin or demote them back to User
  • Assign or remove team/group memberships
  • Suspend a user (they cannot log in; their content is preserved)
  • Delete a user (content is preserved; action is irreversible)

MFA Policy

Your org's MFA policy is either enforced or optional. When it is enforced, every user must set up a TOTP authenticator app on first login before they can continue. When it is optional, org and team admins are shown the setup page on their first login with a Skip for now option, other users are not prompted, and anyone can set up MFA later from their profile. A user who has set up MFA is always asked for a code when they log in, whatever the policy.

Teams

A Team is a named group of users that reflects how your organisation is structured — for example, Engineering, Marketing, or Operations. Teams are primarily organisational labels: they make it easy to see at a glance who belongs where, and they can be used as the basis for wiki space access rules.

Creating a Team

  1. 1
    Admin Console → Teams tab → Create Team.
  2. 2
    Enter a name (e.g., engineering) and an optional description.
  3. 3
    Add members by selecting from the list of existing users.

Assigning Users to Teams

You can add or remove a user from a team from either the Users tab (edit a user → Teams section) or the Teams tab (edit a team → Members section). A user can belong to multiple teams.

Teams vs Groups
Teams are structural labels (who are you?). Groups control what you can access. Typically you'll assign users to both: a Team for their department and one or more Groups that grant the right wiki space access.

Groups & Permissions

A Group is a permission bundle. Each group is associated with one or more Wiki Spaces (folder-level access controls) and defines what members can do in those spaces.

Creating a Group

  1. 1
    Admin Console → Groups tab → Create Group.
  2. 2
    Give it a descriptive name, e.g., engineering-readwrite or all-readonly.
  3. 3
    Add users or entire teams to the group.
  4. 4
    Assign the group to one or more Wiki Spaces with the desired access level.

Wiki Space ACLs

Wiki Spaces are top-level folders in the file tree. Each space can have its own access rules:

Access LevelWhat members can do
ReadView and search documents in this space.
WriteCreate, edit, and delete documents in this space.
AdminAll of the above, plus manage the space itself.

Navigate to Admin Console → Wiki Space ACLs to configure which groups have access to which spaces.

Recommended Starting Configuration

  • Create an all-users group containing everyone → give it Read access to the main wiki space.
  • Create a contributors group for active editors → give it Write access.
  • Create department-specific groups for spaces that contain sensitive departmental content.

API Tokens

API Tokens are how your users' AI assistants authenticate to Elefant's MCP server. Each user creates their own personal tokens in My Settings → API tokens, one for each client. Users do not share tokens. Clients that support OAuth, such as claude.ai and the Claude mobile apps, need no token; their sessions appear in My Settings → OAuth sessions.

Token Permissions

PermissionGrantsDefault
memory_readSearch and list memories✅ On
memory_writeAdd, update and delete memories❌ Off
wiki_readSearch and read wiki pages✅ On
wiki_writeCreate and update pages, only inside the org's AI folder (AI/ by default)✅ On
board_readList cards and columns on the Board✅ On
board_writeAdd, move, edit, comment on and close cards❌ Off

The defaults are those of the API tokens form in My Settings. For most users, also turn on memory_write, so that the assistant can capture, and board_write if the assistant should file cards. The Connect your AI client guide (docs/connect/README.md in the Elefant repository) has the setup for each client.

Tokens are shown once
A token value is shown once at creation and never retrievable again. Instruct users to copy it into their Claude Desktop config immediately. If a token is lost, they can delete it and create a new one.

The Board

The Board tab is a Kanban board for your org's work and ideas. People and AI assistants share it: a person drags a card in the browser, and an assistant moves the same card with the MCP card_* tools. The board updates live for everyone who has it open.

Setting it up

  • Columns belong to the org. Name them for the way your team works, for example Ideas, Up Next, In Progress, Review and Done. Use Add column on the board, and drag columns to reorder them.
  • Labels are free text, for example bug or a sprint name. Agree on a short list, because people and assistants filter by label.
  • A card's repository field records the GitHub repository the work belongs to, so one board can serve several projects.

Cards

A card has a title, a description, comments, attachments, labels, the people on it, and links: to other cards (related, blocks or blocked by) and to wiki pages. Closing a card keeps it, and it can be reopened from the Closed column. Cards are not deleted.

Assistants on the board

An assistant needs board_read to see the board and board_write to change it. With board_write, an assistant files ideas that the user is not building yet as cards, and files a card when the user says "note to self". An assistant that works cards unattended picks the next card whose blockers are done.

Getting Help

If something isn't working or you have questions about your org setup:

  • Getting help: ask whoever runs your Elefant, or ask the community in the project's GitHub Discussions. Include a description of the issue and any error messages you see.
  • User guide: share the companion Elefant User Guide with your team members for help with day-to-day use.
  • Data export: at any time you can export your wiki (Settings → Wiki Backup) and your memories (Settings → Memory Backup) as portable files. Your data is never locked in.
